## The thing that changes the whole plan

**Stage 1 fire restrictions went into effect Aug 7, 2026** across Ada, Boise, Valley, Gem, Canyon, Elmore, Owyhee, Payette, and Washington counties. Under Stage 1:

- Wood/charcoal fires are legal **only** in an agency-installed metal fire ring at a developed recreation site.
- **Dispersed camping means no campfire at all.** Not a small one. Not in a ring you build.
- Propane and LPG devices are **exempt** — legal anywhere cleared of flammable material.

So the "maybe dispersed" idea costs you the fire this weekend. A developed site — reserved *or* first-come — is the only way to get both seclusion and flames under a bacon pan. The two-burner propane stove is doing real work regardless, and dessert needs a plan that isn't strictly s'mores.

## Reading the availability data correctly

Recreation.gov marks a site-night three ways, and conflating two of them gives you the wrong answer:

- **Reserved** — somebody booked it. Gone.
- **Available** — bookable right now.
- **Not Reservable** — *not in the booking system.* At these campgrounds that usually means **first-come, first-served**, often paid on arrival by QR code (recreation.gov calls it "Scan and Pay"). It does **not** mean full.

A site that reads `Not Reservable` on every day of the month is being held out of the system on purpose. That's walk-up inventory, and it's invisible if you only look at the booking calendar.

## Straight-line distance lies out here

Radius searches measure crow-flies distance, which in the Salmon River mountains is close to meaningless — there's a wilderness in the middle and the roads go around it. Routed driving times from Boise:

<div style="overflow-x:auto">

| Campground | Road miles | Driving time | Surface warnings (from the Forest Service's own directions) |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Pine Flats | 74 | **2.5 h** | rough |
| Grouse | 119 | **3.0 h** | winding, gravel, dirt |
| Cozy Cove (Deadwood) | 92 | **3.1 h** | dirt |
| Upper Payette Lake | 125 | **3.3 h** | gravel |
| Buckhorn Bar | 127 | **3.9 h** | — |
| Yellow Pine | 139 | **5.0 h** | rough, narrow |

</div>

**This rules out the walk-up bonanza east of McCall.** Yellow Pine has 14 first-come sites and Buckhorn Bar has 10, and on a map they look close. They're a 4–5 hour drive on rough road. Leaving Boise at 11:00 you'd reach Yellow Pine around 16:00 having spent the entire first day driving. Not this trip.

*Treat these as a ranking, not a stopwatch.* Routed estimates run conservative on mountain highway and badly so on gravel — the router put Grouse→Burgdorf at 1h57 where Google says **1h13** for the same 45 miles. Use the routing to sort destinations; use Google for the number you actually plan around.

### Hot springs near camp — the honest geography

There is no close free spring to Grouse, and that's geology rather than bad luck. Idaho's free primitive springs cluster in two corridors — Warm Lake / South Fork Salmon (Trail Creek, Vulcan, Molly's) and Banks–Lowman (Kirkham, Bonneville, Pine Flats). Goose Lake Road is in neither, and **every free option is further away than Burgdorf**:

| From Grouse | Each way | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Zim's, New Meadows | ~1 h | ~$12, developed pool in town |
| **Burgdorf** | **1h13** | $20, reservation-only |
| Gold Fork, Donnelly | ~1.3 h | paid, developed |
| Trail Creek (free, primitive) | ~2.6 h | free |

Zim's is the only thing meaningfully closer, and it's a town pool rather than a mountain soak. Burgdorf earns its fee here.

**Berries don't justify the drive** — worth being clear about, since it's a tempting rationalization. Goose Lake Road *is* the premier huckleberry corridor; you can pick as well from camp as anywhere up Warren Wagon. The drive buys Burgdorf, not better berries. Making a day of it with picking stops along the way is a fine way to spend Saturday, as long as you know what you're paying for.

## The play: leave at 11, win the walk-up race

An 11:00 departure is the whole advantage here. Checkout is 11:00 AM, so Friday-morning sites are opening up exactly as you're driving. Arrive early afternoon and you're picking from a fresh board, ahead of the after-work Friday wave.

| Target | Arrive | Walk-up sites in play |
|---|---|---:|
| Upper Payette Lake | ~13:45 | 10 |
| Goose Lake Rd (Grouse + Last Chance + Hazard) | ~13:45 | 43 |

Those are good odds, not a coin flip — especially in late August once Idaho schools are back.

### Recommended: Warren Wagon Road, with Grouse booked as insurance

**Book one of the 5 open sites at Grouse right now.** It's cheap certainty in the same region. Then drive to **Upper Payette Lake** first and take a walk-up site if one's there. If it works, you eat a small cancellation fee. If it doesn't, Grouse is 45 minutes away and you already own it.

That's the shape of the whole plan: a bookable floor, and a better ceiling you drive to first.

Why Upper Payette wins on your weights:

- **Hot springs — solved.** **Burgdorf Hot Springs is ~30 minutes further up Warren Wagon Road.** Day soaks are $20/adult in 2-hour blocks (10, 12, 2, 4), **by reservation only, closed Wednesdays.** Book a Saturday afternoon block now. This was the one gap in the Payette option and it closes cleanly.
- **Huckleberries.** Warren Wagon and the Brundage/Goose Lake corridor at 5,900–6,500 ft is the best berry ground within reach of Boise, and late August is the right window up high.
- **Fishing.** Upper Payette is a no-wake lake — good for canoe, kayak, and paddleboard fishing. Secesh River nearby.
- **Beauty and seclusion.** Lake sites at 5,900 ft, sixteen miles past McCall.
- **Swimming.** A real lake that stays full, unlike a drawdown reservoir.

**Fallback chain if Upper Payette is taken:** Jeanette (6 walk-up, close by) → Grouse (yours) → Last Chance and Hazard Lake up Goose Lake Rd (32 walk-up between them).

### Alternate: Deadwood Reservoir — the fishing trip

8 sites bookable across Cozy Cove, River Side, and Barneys, plus ~17 walk-up. Best fishing of any option: kokanee, rainbow, and the Deadwood River. Highest seclusion.

The road complaint has a fix — **take the official approach: Hwy 55 → Banks → Banks-Lowman Hwy → Lowman → Hwy 21 → FR 579 west.** That's the maintained route, not the narrow FS 555 / Scott Mountain track out of Garden Valley that everyone gripes about; the Forest Service specifically discourages trailers on 555. And it puts **Kirkham Hot Springs right on Hwy 21 just east of Lowman**, directly on your drive in — free soak, zero detour.

**The catch:** it's an irrigation reservoir at late-August drawdown. Shoreline can be mud and stumps, which is bad for the swimming and paddleboard half of the weekend. Call the Lowman Ranger District before committing.

### Easy option: Pine Flats

10 sites open, 1h20 from Boise, all pavement, **Pine Flats Hot Springs a short walk from camp** down to the South Fork Payette. But ~3,900 ft means no huckleberries, hot days, and the least seclusion. This is the "make it easy" pick.

## The town half

Warren Wagon Road runs you through McCall in both directions, so the sightseeing is free — no separate trip, just a stop on the way in or out. This is the part that makes it read as a getaway rather than a fishing trip with a girlfriend attached.

**Burgdorf Hot Springs** is the anchor and it's worth treating as the main event, not a detour. It's an 1860s gold-rush stage stop, still standing — weathered log buildings, a big sand-bottomed pool fed straight out of the ground, no chlorine and no chrome. Open for summer soaks **June through October** (winter is snowmobile-only), 10am–6pm, **closed Wednesdays**, $20/adult, and **reservation-only in 2-hour blocks** at 10, 12, 2, and 4. It's about 30 minutes past Upper Payette Lake on gravel. Book the Saturday 2:00 block now — they do sell out, and turning up unbooked means turning around.

**In McCall itself:**

- **Legacy Park** — downtown lakefront, the postcard view of Payette Lake, easy place to sit with a coffee.
- **Ponderosa State Park** — the peninsula out into the lake. Osprey Point overlook is a short walk to a genuinely good view, and there are huckleberries in the park itself if the high patches disappoint. Day-use fee.
- **Downtown** — a walkable few blocks of shops, a bookstore, ice cream. Easy hour.
- **Brundage Mountain** — summer chairlift rides and views out over the Seven Devils, if it's running that weekend.
- **Practical stop:** last real groceries, ice, firewood, and cell service before you go dark. Fuel up here.

A good shape for the weekend: rough camping and berry picking as the substance, Burgdorf on Saturday afternoon as the thing you planned *for her*, and a slow McCall morning on the way home Sunday.

## Fishing — two lakes, two different trips

Idaho Fish and Game stocking records, pulled 2026-08-20. These lakes are not equivalent and it's worth knowing which is which before you pick.

### Goose Lake — 366 acres, on your road

| Stocked | Fish | Size |
|---|---|---|
| **2026-06-24** | 4,001 rainbow (triploid) | **Catchable, 12–14 in** |
| 2025-06-24 | 4,012 rainbow | 12–14 in |
| 2024-06-26 | 2,010 rainbow | 12–14 in |
| 2023-07-05 | 4,977 rainbow | 6+ in |

Four thousand catchable 12–14 inch rainbows went in eight weeks before this trip, and that's the pattern every June. Surveys in 2021 also turned up **brook trout and cutthroat**, so there's a wild component beneath the stockers. Boat ramp, toilet, ADA access.

**This is where the paddleboards go** — 366 acres with a ramp is a real launch, and it's where you catch fish to eat. Saturday morning before the Burgdorf run, or Sunday on the way out. You pass it either direction.

Triploid means sterile: stocked to be caught, not to reproduce. No guilt in keeping a couple for the fire ring, within the limit.

### Twin Lake — 40 acres, a mile off the road

| Stocked | Fish | Size |
|---|---|---|
| 2024-09-23 | 1,001 rainbow (triploid) | **Fry, 0–3 in** |
| 2022-08-26 | 1,000 rainbow | fry |
| 2020-09-11 | 1,000 rainbow | fry |

Fry only, every other year. The 2024 class has had two summers on it, so figure **8–10 inches of wild-grown trout**. Rainbow confirmed in a 2023 survey, brook trout in 2016.

Small fish in a quiet alpine lake — dry-fly water, and the same fishing that worked in the White Clouds. **Elk hair caddis and an Adams, size 18.** This is the Friday hike, not the dinner run.

## Packing — the glamping delta

Everything the ultralight list deliberately leaves at home:

- [ ] Cot or thick air mattress, real pillows, actual blanket
- [ ] Canopy or shade tarp
- [ ] The heavy chairs
- [ ] Two-burner propane stove **+ extra propane** (doing more work than usual under fire restrictions)
- [ ] Cast iron for the bacon
- [ ] **Two coolers** — drinks get opened constantly, food shouldn't. Separating them doubles your ice life.
- [ ] Camp table, string lights, rug for the tent door
- [ ] Bluetooth speaker + charged battery bank
- [ ] Cards, whiskey, glasses that aren't plastic
- [ ] French press or percolator
- [ ] Paddleboards + pump + PFDs
- [ ] **Berry buckets** — rigid, with a handle, not a bag
- [ ] Rods, licenses, flies
- [ ] Water jugs — 5+ gallons
- [ ] Firewood bought locally, plus a lighter that isn't the truck
- [ ] **Cash** — for walk-up fees and Burgdorf
- [ ] **Recreation.gov app downloaded** before you leave McCall — scan-and-pay needs it and there's no service up there

## Notes

- **Bears.** Black bears eat huckleberries too, and you'll be in their aisle. Food in the car overnight.
- **Berry rules.** Personal-use picking on national forest is free; check the ranger district on quantity limits if you plan to fill the truck.
- **Burgdorf is reservation-only** and closed Wednesdays. Book the Saturday block before you go.
- **Smoke.** Idaho was at 19 active fires and National Preparedness Level 5 as of mid-August 2026. Check air quality Thursday — a smoked-in weekend is worth postponing.
- **No cell service** past McCall. Download maps.
- **Check Stage 2.** If restrictions escalate before the 28th, wood fires die everywhere and the cooking plan goes all-propane.
