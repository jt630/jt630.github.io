## The thing that changes the whole plan

**Stage 1 fire restrictions went into effect Aug 7, 2026** across Ada, Boise, Valley, Gem, Canyon, Elmore, Owyhee, Payette, and Washington counties. Under Stage 1:

- Wood/charcoal fires are legal **only** in an agency-installed metal fire ring at a developed recreation site.
- **Dispersed camping means no campfire at all.** Not a small one. Not in a ring you build.
- Propane and LPG devices are **exempt** — legal anywhere cleared of flammable material.

So the "maybe dispersed" idea costs you the fire this particular weekend. That's the argument for booking a developed site: a reserved campground is the only way to get both seclusion *and* flames under a bacon pan. It also means the two-burner propane stove is doing the real work regardless, and dessert needs a plan that isn't strictly s'mores.

### 1. Deadwood Reservoir — the balanced pick

The only option that scores 3.5 or better on all five. Cozy Cove, River Side, and Barneys all have sites open.

The road complaint has a fix. **Take the official approach: Hwy 55 → Banks → Banks-Lowman Hwy → Lowman → Hwy 21 → FR 579 west.** That's the maintained route, not the narrow, beat-up FS 555 / Scott Mountain approach out of Garden Valley that everyone complains about. The Forest Service discourages trailers on 555 specifically; 579 from the Hwy 21 side is the better surface.

And that routing hands you the hot springs for free — **Kirkham is right on Hwy 21 just east of Lowman**, directly on your drive in. Soak Friday afternoon on the way, arrive at camp by evening. Bonneville is a few miles further if Kirkham is a zoo.

- **Fishing:** the strongest of the three. Kokanee and rainbow, plus the Deadwood River.
- **Seclusion:** highest. The long approach filters people out.
- **Watch out:** it's an irrigation reservoir and late August is drawdown season. The shoreline can be mud and stumps, which matters for the swimming and paddleboard half of the plan. Worth a call to the Lowman Ranger District before committing.

### 2. Grouse Campground — if huckleberries are the whole point

5 miles north of McCall on Hwy 55, right on Goose Lake Rd (FR 257) — 4 miles paved, then 6.5 miles gravel. Short dirt, not the Deadwood slog.

This is the **Brundage / Goose Lake corridor at roughly 6,000+ feet, which is the best huckleberry ground within reach of Boise.** Late August is the right window at that elevation — the low patches are done by now, and the high ones are peaking. Fishing at Grouse, Hazard, and Goose Lakes.

The cost is hot springs. Zims is down in New Meadows (developed pool, ~50 min back down) and Burgdorf is a long way around. You'd be picking a berry weekend with a soak as a maybe.

### 3. Pine Flats — the low-effort, hot-spring-forward option

Ten sites open, 1h20 from Boise, all pavement, and **Pine Flats Hot Springs is a short walk from the campground down to the South Fork Payette.** Kirkham and Bonneville are 20–30 minutes east.

But it sits around 3,900 feet: no huckleberries, hot in late August, and it's the least secluded of the three. This is the fallback if the weekend needs to be easy rather than remote.

## Recommendation

**Deadwood, entered via Lowman**, with a Kirkham soak built into the Friday drive. It's the only one that answers all five weights, the fishing is the best of the group, and the hot springs stop costs you nothing because it's on the way.

**Take Grouse instead if huckleberry picking is non-negotiable** — Deadwood's berries are fine, Grouse's are the reason people drive up there.

Either way: **book now.** Five to ten sites, ten days out, on a Friday–Saturday in prime season. That does not survive the week.

## Menu

**Friday dinner** — steak kabobs (skewer them at home, bring in a flat container), foil potatoes in the coals.

**Saturday breakfast** — bacon, pancakes. Plain or blueberry; you haven't picked yet.

**Saturday dinner** — leftover kabob meat, or whatever the fishing produced.

**Sunday breakfast** — **huckleberry pancakes from Saturday's haul.** This is the whole arc. Don't burn the berries on day one.

**All weekend** — seltzers, whiskey, salt crunch (chips, crackers), water.

**Dessert** — a huckleberry crisp in a dutch oven over the fire ring is the move, and it's legal in a developed site. Bring a backup that runs on propane in case restrictions escalate to Stage 2.

## Packing — the glamping delta

Everything the ultralight list deliberately leaves at home:

- [ ] Cot or thick air mattress, real pillows, actual blanket
- [ ] Canopy or shade tarp — no shade at a drawn-down reservoir
- [ ] The heavy chairs
- [ ] Two-burner propane stove **+ extra propane** (it's doing more work than usual under fire restrictions)
- [ ] Cast iron for the bacon
- [ ] **Two coolers** — drinks get opened constantly, food shouldn't. Separating them doubles your ice life.
- [ ] Camp table, string lights, rug for the tent door
- [ ] Bluetooth speaker + charged battery bank
- [ ] Cards, whiskey, glasses that aren't plastic
- [ ] French press or percolator
- [ ] Paddleboards + pump + PFDs
- [ ] **Berry buckets** — something rigid with a handle, not a bag
- [ ] Rods, licenses, flies
- [ ] Water jugs — 5+ gallons, don't count on a spigot
- [ ] Firewood bought locally, plus a lighter that isn't the truck

## Notes

- **Bears.** Black bears eat huckleberries too, and you'll be in their aisle. Food in the car overnight.
- **Berry rules.** Personal-use picking on national forest is free; check the ranger district on quantity limits if you're planning to fill the truck.
- **Smoke.** Idaho was at 19 active fires and National Preparedness Level 5 as of mid-August 2026. Check air quality Thursday before you commit — a smoked-in weekend is worth postponing.
- **No cell service** at any of these. Download maps before Lowman.
- **Check Stage 2.** If restrictions escalate before the 28th, wood fires die everywhere and the whole cooking plan goes propane.
