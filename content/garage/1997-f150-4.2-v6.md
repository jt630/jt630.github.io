Correction from the first pass at this post: a genuine '97 F150 doesn't have
the old 4.9L inline-six — Ford swapped it out for the **4.2L Essex V6** when
the truck got redesigned for the 1997 model year. The I6 was gone by then.
Parts/videos below are for the 4.2L.

## Current punch list

### Feels like it's skipping a beat (misfire you can feel in the seat)
Start with plugs — the Essex V6 is known to be picky about them, and a
lot of owners chasing this exact symptom trace it to worn plugs or
off-brand plugs that don't seal right, with the fix being OEM Motorcraft.
Gap is 0.054" if you're not buying pre-gapped.

If new plugs don't fully clear it, check the wires next — on this engine,
wires from cylinders that fire back-to-back can "crossfire" if they get
routed side by side (the field from one wire induces a spark in its
neighbor), so route them exactly like the old ones came off, not just
neatly. After that, the coil pack is the next suspect.

- Plugs: [Motorcraft SP-504 spark plug (RockAuto)](https://www.rockauto.com/en/parts/motorcraft,SP504,spark+plug,7212)
- Plugs (alt source): [Amazon — Motorcraft SP-504](https://www.amazon.com/Motorcraft-SP-504-Spark-Plug/dp/B000WI66MG)
- Coil pack: [RockAuto — 1997 F-150 4.2L V6 ignition coil](https://www.rockauto.com/en/catalog/ford,1997,f-150,4.2l+v6,1122455,ignition,ignition+coil,7060)
- Install video: [97-03 Ford F-150 4.2L Spark Plugs and Wires](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jLkL1dkNJ8E)

### Driver-side mirror glass rattles in the housing
Third correction on this one — it's the glass itself loose inside the
driver-side door mirror housing, not the housing/mount and not the interior
rearview mirror. On these, the glass just clips/sticks onto a plastic ball
mount inside the shell; that clip or foam pad fails with age and the glass
rattles around loose (and can eventually fall out) while the housing stays
solid on the door. Fix is a glass-only replacement, not the whole mirror
assembly — it pops out and the new one clips (or peels-and-sticks) in.

- Part: [Amazon — flat driver-side mirror glass, 1997-2003 F-150](https://www.amazon.com/1997-2003-F-150-Driver-Mirror-Replacement/dp/B00NJA9CF2)
- Install video: [How to Replace Mirror Glass 1997-2003 Ford F-150 (1A Auto)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ujYonrGpM_Y)

### Belt squeaks
Check the serpentine belt for glazing/cracking first (shiny, hard, or
cracked = replace it, don't just dress it). If the belt looks fine, the
noise is usually the tensioner — either the pulley bearing is going or
spring tension is weak and letting the belt slip under load.

- Belt: [RockAuto — 1997 F-150 4.2L V6 belt](https://www.rockauto.com/en/catalog/ford,1997,f-150,4.2l+v6,1122455,belt+drive,belt,8900)
- Tensioner: [RockAuto — 1997 F-150 4.2L V6 belt tensioner](https://www.rockauto.com/en/catalog/ford,1997,f-150,4.2l+v6,1122455,belt+drive,belt+tensioner,11659)

### AC blowing/freezing solid
Classic symptom of the low-pressure cycling switch failing, or refrigerant
running low so the low side drops below freezing and ices the evaporator
instead of cooling it. The switch threads into a Schrader valve on the
accumulator — no need to discharge the system to swap it.

- Part: [eBay — Motorcraft 52839NKWH A/C pressure switch, 1997-2008 F150](https://www.ebay.com/itm/385818769306)
- Install video: [F-150 A/C Diagnosis & Cycling Switch Replacement](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IA-ydT4fmcE)

### Shifter plastic broken
The column shift lever pivots on a nylon bushing that gets brittle and
snaps with age, giving you sloppy/broken-feeling shifts. Cheap part, no
need to replace the whole shifter or cable.

- Part: [Ford OEM shift tube bushing F3TZ-7L278-A](https://pifparts.com/product/ford-f3tz-7l278-a-bushing-shift-tube-2-pack-genuine/)
- Install video: [1995-2003 Ford F150/F250/F350/Ranger loose shifter fix](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nUJJVq2sS30)
- General walkthrough: [How to Fix a Sloppy Shifter Arm — OBS F150/F250/F350](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DQQfg1HUUpc)

### Burning oil (topping off between changes)
First rule out the scarier cause below — pull the dipstick and check the
oil isn't milky/frothy (that's coolant getting in, not burning, see the
timing cover item below). If the oil just looks like oil but the level
keeps dropping, the two usual suspects on an engine this age are:

- **PCV valve** — cheap, easy, check first. A stuck/clogged PCV lets
  crankcase pressure build up and pushes oil past seals, especially
  noticeable on deceleration/idle. Swapping it is a 2-minute, no-tools job.
- **Valve stem seals** — if the PCV doesn't help, the rubber seals on the
  valve stems have hardened with age and are letting oil past into the
  cylinders. Tell: blue smoke on startup that fades as it warms up. This
  is a bigger job (pulling the heads or using a compressed-air-through-the-
  spark-plug-hole trick to hold the valves shut) — worth a shop quote
  unless you want to take it on yourself.

- Part: [RockAuto — 1997 F-150 4.2L V6 PCV valve](https://www.rockauto.com/en/catalog/ford,1997,f-150,4.2l+v6,1122455,exhaust+&+emission,positive+crankcase+ventilation+(pcv)+connector,11783)
- Install video: [F-150 4.2L V6 PCV Valve Replacement](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zbSzQKdsRlU)

### Tires need air
Not a parts job, just a reminder to actually check pressure with a gauge
instead of eyeballing it — check the sticker on the driver's door jamb for
the truck's actual spec (typically in the 30-35 PSI range on these, but
use the sticker, not this number) and set it cold, before driving.

- Buy: [Amazon — portable 12V tire inflators](https://www.amazon.com/Best-Sellers-Wheel-Tire-Air-Compressors-Inflators/zgbs/automotive/155346011)

## Known issues on '97-'98 4.2L V6 F150s (reference)

- **Timing cover gasket failure (the big one)** — early '97/'98 4.2L trucks
  shipped with a too-thin front cover gasket. It fails and dumps coolant
  straight into the crankcase, which contaminates the oil and can hydro-lock
  a rod out the side of the block if it's not caught. Ford ran a free owner
  notification program (99B29) on this, but that program expired in 2001 —
  it's on you to check now. Symptoms: milky/frothy oil, coolant loss with no
  visible external leak, white exhaust smoke. Pull the dipstick and check
  the oil color before anything else on this list.
- **Lower intake manifold gasket leaks** — same coolant-into-oil failure
  mode as above, different gasket. Worth checking at the same time.
- **Exhaust manifold leaks** — shows up as a ticking noise that's loudest
  cold and fades as the engine warms up.
- **Rust** — cab corners, rear wheel arches, and frame rails are the spots
  to actually get under the truck and look at.
- **Door sag** — door hinge pins/bushings wear and the doors drop over time.
  Fixable with a hinge pin/bushing kit instead of new hinges.
- **Heater core leaks** — coolant smell/fog on the inside of the windshield
  is the tell.
