# Skewer Pop-up — Legal & Permit Research

Research compiled 2026-10-12 from three parallel web-research passes (health,
fire, business/tax/alcohol). **This is research, not legal advice.** Nothing
here was confirmed by a phone call yet. Every claim below came from a web
source; items marked **UNVERIFIED** could not be confirmed from a primary
source. City code (amlegal.com) returned 403 to the agents, so Boise code text
was mostly read through city PDFs and search summaries.

Fees and rules drift — re-check before relying on any number.

## Bottom line

- A pop-up at someone else's venue most likely needs **its own** Central
  District Health (CDH) license, not the host's. No source confirmed this either
  way.
- **Charcoal is not banned.** Boise's food-truck checklist explicitly covers
  solid-fuel cooking. The open question is whether a grease-producing yakitori
  grill needs a hood and suppression system (expensive).
- Safest alcohol plan: **host sells all alcohol under its license; the pop-up
  sells only food.**
- Cottage-food law does **not** apply (on-site, immediate-consumption food).

## 1. Health & food safety (Central District Health)

CDH licenses all food establishments serving TCS (time/temperature control for
safety) food — raw and cooked meat count. Rule: IDAPA 16.02.19.
Sources: <https://cdh.idaho.gov/community-health/food-establishments/food-fees-forms-and-applications>,
<https://files.dfm.idaho.gov/dfm-admin-website/rules/current/16/160219.pdf>.

**Permit options**

| Type | Fee | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Temporary Food Establishment (TFE) | $35 (1 day), $45 (2-3 days), $80 (4+ days or multiple events) | Booth/stand up to 14 consecutive days at one *event*. Apply via Accela ≥30 days ahead. Covers the calendar year for the approved menu. Info sheet: <https://cdh.idaho.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Temporary-Food-Establishment-Information.pdf> |
| Mobile food establishment | $80-$100 + $100 plan review | Apply ≥30 days ahead; needs commissary agreement, layout drawing, inspector meeting, other agencies' approvals, pre-op inspection requested ≥2 weeks ahead. A mobile unit must stay *mobile*; a long welded stationary grill probably doesn't qualify (inference). Packet: <https://cdh.idaho.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Mobile-Food-Establishment-Plan-Review-Packet.pdf> |
| Permanent license | $200 + $100 plan review | ≥30 days ahead. |

**Pop-up under a host's license? NOT VERIFIED.** No CDH page addresses pop-ups
or guest chefs. Related facts: licenses are tied to the operator and not
transferable (Idaho Code 39-1604); a restaurant's permanent license "is not
applicable" to a TFE at an event; CDH is actively pursuing unlicensed TCS
sellers (March 2025 release). Practical read: expect to need your own license.

**Commissary / prep:** no food prep in a home kitchen or storage in a
residence (IDAPA 16.02.19 §620). Mobile units need a District-approved
commissary and a yearly agreement. Whether a host kitchen can be the
commissary is the biggest unknown.

**Training:** at least one certified food manager (CFPM) on staff per the CDH
FAQ — ServSafe class $150, 8 h, valid 5 years (CDH dates listed: Oct 22, Nov
18, 2026; register 208-327-7499). DHW food handler card ($48, 4 h) does *not*
satisfy the manager requirement. The TFE exam is described as meant for
"limited menus"; CDH may demand an accredited exam depending on the menu.

**Raw meat & skewer handling** (Idaho temp manual; rule incorporates the 2013
FDA Food Code — UNVERIFIED whether still current):
- Cook 15 s: poultry 165°F; ground/injected meat 155°F; beef, pork, lamb, fish 145°F.
- Cold hold ≤41°F; hot hold ≥135°F; discard TCS food out of temp >4 cumulative hours.
- Cool 135→70°F in 2 h, then →41°F in 4 h. Thaw only refrigerated, under ≤70°F running water, or as part of cooking.
- Stem thermometer ±2°F, ice-point calibrated.
- Separate raw meats by species and from ready-to-eat; no bare-hand contact with RTE food; inspected sources only (home-prepared food is not approved; "Not For Sale" custom-exempt meat can't be served).
- Consumer advisory required for raw/undercooked animal foods (§360).
- Cured/raw cold pintxo items are not addressed — need CDH review.
- CDH table says outdoor BBQ-grill cooking is acceptable on licensed mobile units (must be covered against pests/debris; commercial/NSF equipment).

**Cottage food law:** not applicable. Excludes restaurants/food trucks and
food "prepared, cooked, or served on-site for immediate consumption."
(Idaho Farm Bureau guide: <https://www.idahofb.org/media/mi1burxb/direct-to-consumer-pamphlet.pdf>.)

## 2. Fire code & open flame (Boise Fire)

Adopted code: 2018 International Fire Code with Boise amendments (City Code
5-12-2; <https://www.cityofboise.org/media/11009/amendments-2018-ifc_final.pdf>).
UNVERIFIED that no newer edition has been adopted. State rule IDAPA 18.08.01
defers to local rules for mobile-food permits and extinguishers.

**Permits (fees effective Oct 1, 2026)** —
<https://www.cityofboise.org/departments/fire/fire-prevention/fire-permits-and-service-fees/>

| Permit | Fee |
|---|---|
| Open Flames and Candles | $145 |
| Mobile Food Preparation Vehicles | $45 (annual, Jan-Dec) |
| Tents/Canopies | $145 |
| Temporary Structures or Uses | $187 |
| Special Event – Fire | $280 |
| LPG (propane) | $187 |

No page states which permit covers a *stationary* charcoal grill. The Open
Flames permit covers "dining areas of restaurants or drinking establishments,"
so it is the likeliest fit for a host patio — that is the agent's inference,
not the city's statement. Apply at permits.cityofboise.org.

**Solid-fuel rules** (Boise mobile-food checklist, a likely benchmark:
<https://www.cityofboise.org/media/13938/mfpchecklist25_website.pdf>)
- 2A:10B:C extinguisher near cooking, plus a **1.5 gal Class K or 2A water extinguisher for solid-fuel operations**; annual service tags.
- 10 ft from buildings, structures, vehicles, combustibles (NFPA 96 guidance Boise links).
- Fuel not stored within 3 ft of the appliance, above heat, or in the ash path.
- Ash removed at least daily into a closed metal container ≥3 ft from the appliance.
- Propane: tanks ≥10 ft from unit and buildings; own LPG permit.
- **Open question:** grease-laden vapor equipment needs a hood + automatic suppression (serviced every 6 months). Fat dripping on charcoal plausibly counts — ask the inspector.

**Air quality:** City Code 5-12-15 bans *open burning* when Idaho DEQ AQI ≥60.
UNVERIFIED whether a cooking grill is "open burning." Foothills WUI areas have
extra rules; downtown is almost certainly not WUI (not stated).

**Tents/canopies:** permit above 400 sq ft with sides, 700 sq ft without; no
numeric cooking clearances on the page.

**Sidewalk/right-of-way:** nothing found on open-flame cooking on public
sidewalks. Downtown vending is regulated under City Code Title 3 Ch. 2 (not
readable by the agents).

**Venue:** leases and liability policies commonly restrict open flame — read
them (general knowledge, no source).

## 3. Business, tax, alcohol, noise

**City Vendor License** — street vendors, solicitors, temporary merchants (≤6
months). Needs Idaho Secretary of State registration; bond $1,000/person or
$5,000/company; no fee or insurance requirement listed. Downtown-core vending
must use authorized street-vending **medallions** (City Clerk). Source:
<https://www.cityofboise.org/departments/city-clerk/licensing/other/vendor-license/>.
In March 2024 the council dropped the separate city mobile-food license as
duplicative of CDH
(<https://www.boisestatepublicradio.org/news/2024-03-28/city-of-boise-drops-duplicative-licensing-requirements-for-food-trucks-and-market-vendors>);
**UNVERIFIED** whether the Vendor License + bond still apply to a food pop-up
on private property.

**Special Event Permit:** only for public-property events >1,000 attendees or
needing city resources; 60 days' lead; $1M CGL insurance. Probably N/A.

**Zoning:** nothing found on temporary outdoor food sales on private downtown
property. UNVERIFIED.

**Sales tax:** Idaho Tax Commission covers prepared food from food
trucks/sidewalk vendors (<https://tax.idaho.gov/taxes/sales-use/guides-for-certain-groups/food/food-drinks/>).
Rate (secondary sources: 6%) UNVERIFIED on primary site. Temporary seller's
permit: infrequent sales, max 3/year (<https://tax.idaho.gov/taxes/sales-use/permits/temporary-sellers-permits/>);
a weekly pop-up likely needs a regular permit — UNCERTAIN.

**Entity:** sole prop is simplest; an LLC separates personal assets but doesn't
replace insurance (~$100 filing per third-party sites, UNVERIFIED). Talk to a
lawyer/CPA.

**Alcohol:** a pop-up cannot sell alcohol without its own license; "dry hire"
pouring of someone else's alcohol is a violation (unverified current statute).
State bans beer sales 1-6 a.m.; locals may extend to 2 a.m. (Idaho Code
23-1012). Boise's actual hours NOT FOUND.

**Noise:** City Code 6-20 bars amplification plainly audible inside a
residence not the source. No verified decibel/time limit.

## Open decisions this research surfaces

1. **License path:** TFE (cheap, but defined per *event*) vs mobile vs
   something CDH invents for recurring pop-ups. Answer decides lead time and cost.
2. **Hood/suppression for the grill** — could change which grill is viable or
   whether a covered outdoor patio is the only option.
3. **Grill form factor:** CDH mobile rules want a *mobile* unit; a fixed welded
   grill may need to be on a wheeled cart or trailer.
4. **Venue:** a host with a licensed kitchen (commissary, insurance, alcohol
   handled by host) removes the most unknowns.
