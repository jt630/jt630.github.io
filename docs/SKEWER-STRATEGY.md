# Skewer Pop-up — Entity, Market & Path to Launch

Synthesis of three research passes on 2026-10-12 (entity form, market/venues,
commissary/funding), built on `docs/SKEWER-LEGAL.md`. **Research, not legal or
tax advice.** Source quality is mixed: Boise code (amlegal) returned 403 to the
agents, the market pass read search snippets only (nothing tagged VERIFIED), and
many dollar figures are estimates. Tags: V = official page, S = secondary
(vendor/blog/news), U = unverified/inference.

## The answer in five lines

1. **Form:** single-member LLC before the first service; default tax treatment;
   insurance regardless. S-corp only once profit is well past a reasonable
   salary (~$60-80k net is the agent's *estimate*, U).
2. **Play:** a kitchenless downtown taproom/cidery patio, Thu-Sat ~9 PM-1 AM,
   ideally a host whose licensed kitchen can double as your commissary.
3. **Win:** own the 10 PM-1 AM slot (the only late alternatives found were
   pizza, calzone, pub food), a tight 6-8 skewer menu, and Basque ingredients
   in the tare so it isn't "just yakitori."
4. **Over the line:** a CDH license path, a commissary, a grill the inspectors
   accept, and a liability policy. These need phone calls, not more research.
5. **Season:** run Oct-Nov, pause or go indoors Dec-Feb, relaunch March
   (Treefort week).

## 1. Entity form

**Recommendation:** single-member LLC now. Cooking over fire for late-night
crowds is real burn/fire/foodborne-illness exposure, hosts usually want a named
insured with a certificate of insurance, and the cost is small.

| Item | Detail | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| LLC Certificate of Organization | ~$100 online (~$120 paper) | S |
| Annual report | sources conflict: free vs $100-120. Check sosbiz.idaho.gov | S/U |
| Registered agent | required; you can be your own (Idaho street address); services ~$125/yr | V/S |
| Assumed business name (DBA) | ~$25; an LLC trading under the skewer brand needs one | S |
| EIN | free at irs.gov | V |
| Idaho income tax | flat 5.3% for tax year 2025; 2026/2027 rate depends on bill H0589 (status unconfirmed) | V |
| Sales tax | 6% state; prepared meals taxable; register the seller's permit under the LLC/EIN | S |
| Workers' comp | owner/working LLC member exempt (Idaho Code 72-212(6)); employees covered from first hire; hosts may still demand it | V/S |
| Employees | Idaho Dept of Labor registration, withholding, new-hire reporting (S) | S |

**Decision tree**
- One-off, no host requirements, fine with personal exposure → stay sole prop
  (still buy liability insurance).
- Host asks for a COI/indemnity, open flame, or recurring → **single-member LLC**.
- Partner/investor arrives → multi-member LLC + written operating agreement.
- Net profit consistently well above a reasonable salary → ask a CPA about the
  S election (Form 2553). Idaho adds a $20 minimum tax + $10 building fund on
  corporations including S corps (V).
- Raising institutional equity → lawyer, C-corp.

**An LLC only works if** you keep money separate, sign as the LLC, and carry
insurance. It doesn't cover your own negligence or personal guarantees.

**Insurance (estimates, national not Idaho):** hosts typically ask for
general liability $1M/$2M with products coverage and additional-insured status;
fire/cooking endorsement likely. National food-truck GL medians run ~$350-$1,700
a year (S); the agent's working estimate for event-only is **$400-1,200/yr
(U)**. Get 2-3 Idaho quotes.

**Alcohol exposure:** Idaho limits dram-shop/social-host liability by statute
(Idaho Code 23-808, V). If the host holds the liquor license and sells all
alcohol and you sell only food, your liquor exposure is small. Don't sell or
comp drinks.

**Host agreement:** a license agreement (permission to use space, no
exclusive possession) is the common pop-up form; rent or a lease can create
tenant obligations and may conflict with the host's own lease or licenses. A
revenue share is a payment term, not its own category, but shared profit and
control can drift toward partnership, so sign as the LLC and state "no
partnership." Cover: who holds the food license, indemnity (try mutual),
insurance wording, fire/hood requirements, termination notice, sales tax
collection, alcohol. Idaho-specific landlord-tenant treatment: U.

**Basque angle:** pintxos and yakitori share the small skewered bar-snack idea.
Risk: heritage institutions on the Basque Block may read a careless "Basque"
label as appropriation. No source on community reaction exists. Mitigations

Museum/Center, have a Basque-American contact review the concept, and stay off
the Block itself until invited.

## 3. How to win (hypotheses to test, not findings)

- Own 10 PM-1 AM Thu-Sat.
- 6-8 skewers + 1-2 cold pintxo items. Price skewers ~$4-7 (U; a 2018 Txikiteo
  anchor of $2-3 pintxos suggests low local pintxo pricing).
- Basque ingredients in the tare/seasoning as the differentiator.
- Charcoal theater at the patio — only if fire/health rules allow.
- These map directly onto the success criteria in `SKEWER-POPUP.md`; test them
  with nights.csv rather than assuming.

**Season:** Dec-Feb Boise highs are ~38°F, lows ~25°F (S). Outdoor charcoal then
needs covered or indoor space, which runs into fire/health rules. Plan Oct-Nov,
then March onward; consider an indoor residency or skip the cold months.

## 4. What takes us over the line

Nothing found lightens the path for made-to-order skewers. Idaho SB 1283
(cottage/"direct-to-consumer," effective March 20, 2026) covers packaged,
mostly non-meat foods and reportedly excludes made-to-order items (S). Boise
dropped its separate mobile food license in March 2024 (V), but whether the
Vendor License + bond + downtown medallions still apply to a private-property
pop-up is **unconfirmed** — one market-pass source still listed an "Eating &
Drinking Mobile" license, which conflicts with the repeal; treat as stale and
ask the Clerk.

**Ordered by lead time**
1. **CDH call first** — TFE vs mobile vs host-license path for a recurring
   pop-up; whether a fixed welded charcoal grill is acceptable; which
   commissary they'd accept. (Plan review $100; mobile $80-100; TFE $35-80.)
2. **Commissary or host-kitchen agreement** (CDH needs it). Options found, all
   unconfirmed: Aire Kitchen (W Ustick, ~$25/hr listing, page now 404), W Main
   St industrial kitchens, West End Food, Osage Kitchen, The Packing Shed. A
   licensed restaurant with a slow night is a fallback. Ask each about
   charcoal, ventilation, raw poultry handling, cold storage.
3. **Grill** — order/fabricate (weeks). New restaurant robata ~$19,500 (S); a
   custom stainless trough *guess* is $1,500-4,000 (U). No local fabricator
   found. CDH wants commercial/NSF-type equipment; custom welded acceptance is
   unconfirmed.
4. **Boise Fire** — permit type and hood/suppression answer, extinguishers
   (see SKEWER-LEGAL.md). Open Flames $145, Mobile Food $45.
5. **City Clerk** — Vendor License/bond/medallion status.
6. **ServSafe ($150) and an insurance quote.**
7. **Free advice anytime:** Idaho SBDC (Boise State), SCORE Boise. Microloans
   (EO, MoFi, others, S) only if the grill goes new. No open Boise grant found
   (the ARPA mini-grant closed Jan 2023).
8. **Supplies last:** Cash & Carry (public access unconfirmed), Basque Market
   (retail), Anderson Reserve butcher (S). Binchotan ~$8-15/kg (S).

**Rough startup budget (mostly estimates):** ~$4,500-9,000 lean with a modest
grill; $20K+ with a new commercial grill. Commissary ~$200-400 per prep event
at $25/hr.

## Suggested sequence

1. Calls: CDH, Boise Fire Prevention, City Clerk (numbers in SKEWER-LEGAL.md).
2. Pick a host from the venue list and ask: kitchen status, charcoal OK,
   insurance minimum, fee/split, license agreement.
3. Form the LLC, EIN, DBA, bank account, insurance, seller's permit.
4. Commissary/host-kitchen agreement; CDH application (≥30 days lead).
5. Grill + fire permits + extinguishers.
6. First nights in Nov (shoulder season) if lead times allow, else March.
